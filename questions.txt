TALAT — INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
===========================

ARCHITECTURE & DESIGN
----------------------
1.  Walk me through the overall architecture of Talat from top to bottom. What are the main components and how do they communicate?

2.  Why is the matching engine single-threaded? What does that design decision buy you, and what does it cost?

3.  You explicitly traded off performance for readability in places. Give me two concrete examples of a choice you made that hurt performance but improved clarity.

4.  You're using C++20. Which specific features from C++20 do you actually use in this project, and why did each one help?

5.  If you needed to add a second tradable instrument (say ETH alongside BTC), how would you extend the architecture? Where does it get complicated?


MATCHING ENGINE
---------------
6.  Walk me through exactly what happens when a user types "buy 5 @ 64000" and presses enter. Trace it through every layer.

7.  You wrap submit and cancel commands in a std::variant<SubmitCmd, CancelCmd>. Why a variant instead of a base class with virtual dispatch? What's the tradeoff?

8.  What is the moodycamel ConcurrentQueue? How does a lock-free MPSC queue work internally at a high level, and why did you choose it over a std::queue protected by a mutex?

9.  The matcher runs a tight bulk_dequeue loop on one thread. What happens to submitting threads while the matcher is busy? Do they block?

10. Explain GTC, IOC, and FOK semantics. When would a real trader use each one?

11. How do stop orders work? When are they triggered, and how does the matcher know to check them?

12. What is self-match prevention? Why does it exist, and what would happen if you removed it?

13. Partial fills: you track filled_qty_ in a separate unordered_map<OrderId, Quantity> on the matcher rather than inside the order struct. Why?

14. How does the matcher measure order processing latency, and what exactly does that latency represent? What is and isn't included in the measurement?


ORDER BOOK
----------
15. Why boost::container::flat_map instead of std::map for the price levels? Walk me through the performance difference.

16. What is the time complexity of add_order, cancel_order, and best_bid? Are there any gotchas?

17. Bids are stored in a flat_map sorted ascending, but you access them with rbegin→rend. Why not just store them descending and walk forward?

18. PriceLevel uses std::list<OrderId> for the queue within a level. What property of std::list makes it appropriate here? What does it cost you?

19. What is price-time priority? How does your book enforce it, specifically?

20. You have a separate unordered_map<OrderId, LimitOrder> alongside the price-level maps. Why not embed the full LimitOrder inside PriceLevel?

21. After all orders at a price level cancel, what happens to that entry in the flat_map? Is that a problem?


CONCURRENCY & SYNCHRONIZATION
------------------------------
22. The OrderBook uses a shared_mutex. Describe the reader/writer pattern: who takes shared locks, who takes exclusive locks, and why?

23. What is the "snapshot-before-callback" pattern in the walk methods? Why is it there and what deadlock does it prevent?

24. Explain acquire and release memory ordering. LatentPrice uses memory_order_release on store and memory_order_acquire on load — what guarantee does that give you, and what would break if you used relaxed on both?

25. What is a data race in the C++ memory model? How does TSan detect one?

26. TSan passed. Does that mean the code is race-free? Explain your confidence level.

27. The EventBus publish takes a shared lock but subscribe takes a unique (exclusive) lock. Why the asymmetry?

28. next_token_ in EventBus uses memory_order_relaxed on fetch_add. Is that safe? Why?


EVENTBUS
--------
29. How does EventBus achieve type-safe event dispatch when the handler map is keyed by std::type_index and stores std::any?

30. What is std::type_index? How does it identify a type at runtime?

31. What overhead does std::any introduce compared to a typed pointer? In what scenario would that matter?

32. What is a SubscriptionToken and why does subscribe return one? What happens if you don't unsubscribe before the subscriber is destroyed?


MARKET MICROSTRUCTURE / FINANCIAL
-----------------------------------
33. Explain the Glosten-Milgrom adverse selection model in plain language. How does your MarketMaker implement it, and what behavior does it produce?

34. What is Geometric Brownian Motion? Why do you model the fair value with zero drift instead of some positive expected return?

35. InformedTrader submits IOC limit orders instead of GTC. Why? What would change if it used GTC?

36. What would happen to price dynamics if you removed NoiseTrader entirely and only had MarketMaker + InformedTrader?

37. What is Poisson arrival modeling for order flow? Why is it a more realistic model than "one order per tick"?

38. Prices are stored as uint64_t fixed-point (10000 = $1.00) instead of double. Why? What specific problem does floating-point cause in financial calculations?


PROFILING & SYSTEMS
--------------------
39. We ran perf stat and saw IPC of ~0.55. What does IPC mean, and what does a value of 0.55 tell you about this workload?

40. Cache miss rate came out at 25–29%. What causes that in this codebase, and is it a problem given the design goals?

41. perf stat showed sys time exceeded user time (34% of wall time in kernel). What tools did you use to investigate, and what did you actually find?

42. What are AddressSanitizer, UBSanitizer, and ThreadSanitizer? What does each one check, and how does each one work mechanically?

43. Why must TSan and ASan be compiled separately — why can't you combine them into one build?

44. What is a flame graph? How do you generate one on Linux and what does it show you?

45. What is perf c2c, and why would it be particularly interesting for this codebase?

46. If you needed to cut p99 latency from 166µs to under 10µs to be competitive with a production exchange, list the first three changes you'd make and why.
